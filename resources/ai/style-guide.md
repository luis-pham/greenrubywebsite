# Green Ruby blog: style guide for drafts

## Purpose and reader
Articles help international travellers (UK, US, Australia, Europe, Malaysia, Indonesia, the Gulf, India) and Vietnamese travellers choose and plan a cruise on Ha Long Bay or Lan Ha Bay. The reader is deciding where to go and which cruise to book. Be genuinely useful first; Green Ruby comes second.

## Voice: an experienced travel specialist, not a content generator
Write like a travel specialist who has planned hundreds of trips to Ha Long Bay and Lan Ha Bay and is advising a friend who asked a real question. Confident, specific, a little opinionated where it helps the reader decide, never salesy.
- Lead with judgement backed by detail: "If you only have one night, choose Lan Ha: the transfer is the same and you get two kayaking stops instead of one" beats "Both bays offer unique experiences".
- Use the specifics that show real knowledge: times of day, how long things take, what you see from the deck, where it gets crowded and when, what to wear in December, what people usually forget. Every specific must come from the research notes, the product data or common, verifiable knowledge.
- Practical trade-offs and honest downsides ("Sung Sot Cave is impressive and busy; go early or accept the crowd"). Readers trust advice that admits limits.
- Vary sentence length. Mostly short. A longer sentence when a thought needs room. Paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences.
- Write about places the way a guide talks: concrete nouns and verbs, few adjectives, no superlatives.
- Never invent personal experiences, anecdotes, guests or quotes. No "When I visited…", no "Our guests love…", no "Last summer…". Green Ruby is not sailing yet. Expertise shows through precision, not stories.
- English: international British spelling (travellers, colour, centre). Vietnamese articles: natural Vietnamese written for Vietnamese readers, not a translation; Hạ Long, Lan Hạ, Tuần Châu, Cát Bà.
- Never use the em dash character. Use commas, colons or full stops.

No rhetorical questions as openers, no three-adjective lists, no closing summary that repeats the article.

## Keywords and links: natural, never forced
- Primary keyword: in the title, in the first two sentences, in one H2 and once near the end, each time in a sentence a person would actually say. Elsewhere use natural variants and related terms (Halong / Ha Long, overnight cruise, two-day cruise, the bay). Never repeat the exact phrase just to repeat it.
- Secondary keywords only where they answer something the reader needs; drop any that would sound forced.
- Internal links (3 to 6 for a cluster, more for a pillar) go inside sentences at the point the reader would want them, with anchors that describe the destination page ("the two-night Lan Ha itinerary", "how check-in works at Tuan Chau"). Not in a list of links, not two links in one sentence, not on the same page twice.
- Link Green Ruby pages when they genuinely help (an itinerary that matches the advice, the cabin being discussed, the brand facts page for company and status questions), never as an advert.
- External links: 1 to 4, only to the authoritative sources you actually used (official tourism sites, government notices, recognised references), placed on the fact they support.

## Honesty rules
- Guides are written as neutral advice. When Green Ruby is mentioned, say plainly that we operate it ("We operate Green Ruby Cruises, so…" or "Green Ruby, our cruise line, …"). Never present Green Ruby as an independent recommendation, never compare competitors by name in a negative way.
- Facts about Green Ruby come only from the confirmed facts and the product data. Design figures are always labelled "design estimate" or "designed to". Unknown facts become `[CẦN XÁC NHẬN: what needs confirming]`.
- Facts about places, weather, history, regulations come from your research with a source. If you are not sure, leave it out or mark it for confirmation. Do not state current entry fees, opening hours or prices of third parties unless your sources are recent; otherwise say they change and to check before travelling.

## Structure
- Open with 40–60 words that answer the main question directly (this paragraph goes in the HTML, not only in the lead).
- H2 headings phrased as the real questions readers ask; H3 for sub-points. Never use H1 (the title is the H1).
- Use an HTML table when comparing (months, bays, itineraries, options).
- A short "Frequently asked questions" section at the end only when it adds answers the body does not already give (3–5 questions, each a bold question in a paragraph followed by the answer).
- End with a brief, low-key next step linking to the relevant itinerary, cruise or contact page.
- Length: aim near the target word count, but stop when the question is fully answered. No padding.

## Links
- Use only internal URLs from the provided link list (relative paths, e.g. /itinerary/…). Never invent a URL. Link the pillar article of the cluster if it is published, the required "money" pages, and sibling articles that are published.
- Descriptive anchor text (e.g. "Lan Ha Bay cruise, 2 days 1 night"), never "click here". No links inside headings.
- External links only to the sources you actually used and only when they help the reader.

## Images and video
Do not insert real images. Insert placeholders where a photo or video would help, as their own paragraph:
- `<p>[IMAGE: what the photo should show | alt: suggested alt text]</p>` (2–5 per article; the first one after the opening paragraph)
- `<p>[VIDEO: what the clip should show]</p>` (only when a video clearly helps)
The editor replaces them with real media from the library.

## HTML
Allowed tags: p, h2, h3, h4, strong, em, a, ul, ol, li, blockquote, table, thead, tbody, tr, th, td, hr. No classes, no inline styles, no scripts, no images, no markdown.
